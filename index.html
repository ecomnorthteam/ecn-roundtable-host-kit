02

Host scripts

Say these close to verbatim. The opener sets safety and states the one rule; the breakout cues keep every room on the same clock; the close lands on belonging + a commitment.

0:00 Opening · consent to the intent ›

“Welcome, everyone. Quick note on why we run roundtables instead of another office hour: this room has more combined operator experience than any course you could buy. We’re here today so that you can get unstuck on something real, by borrowing the brains of three other founders who get it.”

“Here’s how it works: I’ll put you in trios. You’ll warm up with one quick question, then each of you gets the hot seat for six minutes.”

One rule, and it’s the whole game: when it’s someone’s turn, the other two ask curious questions first — no advice for the first two minutes. The best help is a great question. You’ll have time to give advice after.”

“Everything is challenge by choice — share as much or as little as you want. Let’s go.”

0:06 Breakout cues · broadcast to all rooms ›

Send each line with Zoom “broadcast message to all rooms.” The live timer surfaces these automatically while the breakout runs.

+0:00START. Connection question first (4 min) — everyone answers.
+4:00HOT SEAT. Person 1: 1 min on what you’re stuck on. Others: clarifying questions ONLY.
+7:00Person 1: open the floor for advice now (3 min).
+10:00SWITCH to Person 2. 1 min stuck → 2 min questions only → 3 min advice.
+16:00SWITCH to Person 3. Same flow.
+22:002 min left — pick the ONE best nugget your trio heard.
0:50 Closing · commitment + group anthem ›

“Two things in the chat before we go:”

1. One commitment — ‘By next week I will ___.’
2. One closing line that starts with ‘I am…’, ‘I believe…’, or ‘I will…’

“I’ll post today’s Gold doc in Slack tonight so nothing gets lost. Thank you for being generous with the room. See you at the social.”

04

If something goes sideways

Pre-answered so a host running this cold never freezes.

Odd numbers

Trios of 3 are ideal. If the count doesn’t divide by 3, make one group of 4 before you ever make a group of 2. Never leave someone alone. 11 → 3+4+4. 13 → 3+3+3+4.

The host isn’t Li

This kit is verbatim-runnable. Your only job: keep time and open warmly. You’re the thread-starter, not the thread-solver. Don’t answer the business questions yourself.

Someone dominates

The timed hot-seat + broadcast cues handle it automatically. If a room runs long, the room-close countdown bails you out.

Low turnout (6–8)

Run two trios, everything else identical. Harvest is faster, so spend the extra minutes on open floor.

A quiet room

The on-screen prompt is the rescue. Tell rooms at the start: “read the prompt out loud if it gets quiet.”

The Gold doc

One nugget per room, typed live into a doc during harvest. That doc is the mined gold — post it to Slack within 24h. Over a year it becomes a searchable founder-wisdom archive.

05

Why it’s built this way

BNI structure + Chad Littlefield facilitation, mapped to the gaps in the April 2026 community audit. Skip unless curious.

01Connection before content›
The 4-minute warm-up comes before business. You can’t change how someone thinks until you change how they feel.
02The 5% rule — activate the larynx›
People who speak in the first 5% of a gathering stay far more engaged. Putting the opener inside the trio (not the room of 15) means everyone speaks in minute one, with no stage fright.
03Trios, not pairs or big groups›
Three is intimate enough for safety, with a buffer if one person’s having an off day. The audit flagged that every current format is full-group, where intimidation thrives.
04Clarifying questions before advice›
The single mechanic that actually mines the gold. Without it, the loudest person solves nothing in minute one. The best help is a great question.
05The 7:1 contribution ratio›
In a 60-minute pure-peer roundtable the members talk ~90% of the time. The host talks for about 8 minutes total. You’re the host, not the sage on the stage.
06Primacy & recency›
People remember the first and last moments, so we bookend deliberately: a warm consent-to-the-intent open, and a commitment + “I am / I believe / I will” close. No fizzle.
07Painkiller over vitamin›
The business prompt targets a real stuck problem, not bonding for its own sake. Both outcomes are structural, not left to chance: belonging (opener + close) and unstuck (hot-seat).

Pick a theme + two prompts (2 min). Open warm, state the one rule. Trios of 3 for 24 minutes: warm-up, then timed hot-seats with questions before advice. Harvest one nugget per room into a doc. Close with a commitment and an “I will.” Post the doc to Slack. Done in 60.
